\chapter{Evaluation and Results}

The effectiveness of the VoiceScore system is quantified by its ability to accurately recognize fine-grained phonetic variations and generalize across diverse, unseen Indian English accents without representation collapse.

\section{Validation Metrics}
During the Session 2 (v3) training phase, the model was evaluated periodically against a held-out validation split drawn from the multi-accent mixture. The primary evaluation metric was the Phoneme Error Rate (PER), defined similarly to Word Error Rate (WER) but calculated at the phonetic token level:
\begin{equation}
    PER = \frac{S + D + I}{N}
\end{equation}
Where $S$ is the number of substitutions, $D$ is deletions, $I$ is insertions, and $N$ is the total number of phonemes in the reference transcription.

At step 41,000 (early stopping point), the model achieved a \textbf{Validation Mean PER of 14.17\%}. This represents a highly competitive phonetic recognition capability, particularly given the narrow distinctions required by the Indian English IPA (e.g., distinguishing an alveolar /t/ from a retroflex /ʈ/).

\section{Generalization Test Suite}
To rigorously test the model's robustness against regional acoustic variations, it was evaluated against a massive, unseen test suite comprising 17,808 samples. This test set explicitly included heavy Tamil, Telugu, Hindi, and Bengali accent variations, which were underrepresented in the baseline NPTEL corpus.

The results of the test suite evaluation are summarized below:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mean PER:} 24.96\%
    \item \textbf{Median PER:} 16.28\%
    \item \textbf{Blank Output Collapse Rate:} 0.00\%
\end{itemize}

The large discrepancy between the Mean (24.96\%) and Median (16.28\%) PER indicates that the vast majority of inferences performed exceptionally well (clustering around the 16\% mark), while a small minority of severe outliers (likely due to extreme background noise or entirely clipped audio files in the test suite) skewed the arithmetic mean upwards. 

\section{Elimination of OOV Failures}
The most significant qualitative result of the Session 2 upgrade was the complete elimination of Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV) drops. By expanding the dictionary to 133,737 words and integrating the neural G2P fallback, the system successfully parsed 100\% of the 17,808 test transcripts into valid phonetic targets. Consequently, the model encountered exactly zero `<unk>` (unknown) token drops, ensuring that the Contrastive CTC aligner could always generate valid frame-level probabilities for the Goodness of Pronunciation (GoP) scorer.

\section{Anti-Collapse Verification}
A critical failure mode in earlier experimental models was representation collapse, where the model would default to predicting the highly frequent schwa (`/ə/`) or the CTC blank token across the entire audio frame to artificially minimize the loss.

Analysis of the output logits over the test suite confirmed that the Schwa Down-weighting mechanism successfully prevented this. The model demonstrated a 0.00\% blank output collapse rate, outputting diverse, high-confidence probability spikes across the full 60-token vocabulary.
